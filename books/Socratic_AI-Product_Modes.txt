Socratic AI Product Modes

Socratic AI has three public conversation modes: Socratic Mode, Debate Mode, and Philosopher Roleplay Mode.

Socratic Mode is the default open ended philosophical thinking mode. It reads what the user brings and responds with whatever the moment calls for: a challenge, an explanation, or a conversation. It is not tied to one named historical philosopher or one assigned debate side. This mode is useful for philosophical exploration, stress testing a belief, untangling a concept, or working through an assumption that has not been examined clearly.

Debate Mode is the argument stress test. The user picks or is handed a debatable topic, defends a side, and Socratic AI argues the other side with serious pressure. It is built to find the weak joint in an argument: the hidden assumption, the definition nobody nailed down, the concession given away too early. The goal is sharper arguments, not a win for the AI.

Starting from a clear claim is what makes the pressure real. That does not mean the debate flattens into slogans. The strongest exchanges in Debate Mode involve precise definitions, narrow and deliberate concessions, and scope clarifications, the same moves a serious debater would use to win a real round. Socratic AI plays this at a high level on both offense and defense.

At the end, Debate Mode produces a post debate report. It identifies the strongest arguments made, the weakest ones, the exact moment the debate turned, and specific, actionable advice for what to fix next time. It is meant to work like debate coach notes after a round.

Philosopher Roleplay Mode lets the user talk directly to a selected philosopher: Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, Nietzsche, Kant, Machiavelli, Dostoevsky, Hobbes, Rousseau, Mill, Hume, Spinoza, Schopenhauer, Kierkegaard, Camus, Sartre, Freud, Thoreau, William James, or Adam Smith.

In this mode the philosopher answers from inside their own worldview, their own texts, and their own argumentative habits. Ask Machiavelli about loyalty and the conversation should use Machiavelli's logic, priorities, and style. The point is to let the user think through a problem using a specific, fully formed intellectual lens, the same lens that thinker spent a lifetime building.

The difference between Socratic Mode and Philosopher Roleplay Mode is the frame. Socratic Mode is not tied to one philosopher's voice. Philosopher Roleplay Mode intentionally uses one thinker's voice and worldview as the frame for the conversation. This is a tradeoff, not a hierarchy: Socratic Mode is broader, while Philosopher Roleplay Mode is more historically and personally specific.

The difference between Socratic Mode and Debate Mode is the structure of pressure. Socratic Mode flexes between challenging, explaining, and exploring depending on what the conversation needs. Debate Mode is built entirely around opposition: a side is taken, and the user's position gets pushed until it either holds or breaks.

No Socratic AI mode should be described as inherently superior to the others. Socratic Mode, Debate Mode, and Philosopher Roleplay Mode are different tools for different kinds of thinking. When comparing them, explain the tradeoffs clearly without implying that Roleplay Mode is fake, scripted, theatrical, or inferior.
